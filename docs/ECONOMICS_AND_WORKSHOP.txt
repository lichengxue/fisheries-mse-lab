PEOPLE & VALUE: A CLASSROOM MSE WORKSHOP
Interface 1.5.0 / age-structured model 1.3.0

Open section 6, People & value. The Economics & livelihoods and Stakeholder
workshop buttons switch between accounting outcomes and deliberation tools.
Use the settings panel (or Edit people & value settings on smaller screens).

WHAT CHANGES THE POPULATION?
The age-structured model, historical F series, Beverton-Holt recruitment,
observation model, estimator and HCR determine each biological future. This
extension retains actual catch and F for every future and every projection year.
Economic assumptions and stakeholder preferences are applied afterward. They
do not alter population dynamics, fleet behavior, or how the HCR sets fishing.

ECONOMIC ACCOUNTING
All dollar values are illustrative classroom assumptions, not empirical fleet
estimates. Change them to examine how conclusions depend on assumptions.

Revenue = actual landed tonnes x 1,000 x landed price per kilogram.
The small-vessel allocation is a fraction of actual catch; the large-vessel
allocation is the remainder. Both use the same price and stock/selectivity.

For an allocated fleet segment:
  cost = annual fixed cost + variable-cost multiplier x cost per unit F
         x actual annual F x allocated share.
An unallocated segment (share = 0) incurs no costs. Otherwise fixed costs remain
even during closures. F is an effort proxy, not a vessel-days observation.

Operating profit = revenue - fishing cost. It excludes the public survey cost.
Annual survey cost = number of projection survey visits x cost per visit / years.
The separate net-after-survey figure subtracts that agency cost from profit.

Supported job-years = segment landed tonnes / segment tonnes per job-year.
This is a simple landings-supported livelihoods proxy, not an employment
forecast. It omits household income, seasonality and within-segment inequality.

NPV = sum over years of profit / (1 + discount rate)^year; year 1 is discounted
once. Metrics average every year and replicate, not just a representative path.
Loss years is the fraction of replicate-years with profit < 0. Negative NPV is
the fraction of futures with discounted total profit < 0. The NPV and annual
profit 10th-90th percentiles describe variation among simulated futures.

The shock matrix compares current assumptions, 1.5 times variable costs (capped
at the control maximum of 5), and 0.75 times price. Biology stays fixed. The
accounting omits price/effort feedback, fleet entry/exit, investment, demand,
subsidies, processing, recreational values and ecosystem-service valuation.

STAKEHOLDER OBJECTIVES
Four default classroom role cards illustrate differing priorities. These are
editable prompts, not statements about the views of real stakeholder groups.
Rename roles, add up to two more, remove roles, or exclude a role from scores.

Set each objective weight from 0 to 10, role influence from 0 to 10, and a
minimum acceptable score from 0 to 100. A role with no positive objective weights
has no valid score. An excluded role or one with zero influence is omitted from
calculated acceptance and overall scores. Manual ballots remain separate.

Each raw outcome is converted to a 0-100 objective score:
  Safety = 100 x (1 - probability of crossing the biomass limit at least once).
  Profit = 100 x clamp(mean annual operating profit / profit target, 0, 1).
  Livelihoods = 100 x clamp(mean supported job-years / livelihoods target, 0, 1).
  Catch = 100 x clamp(mean annual actual catch / catch target, 0, 1).
  Stability = 100 x clamp(1 - mean absolute annual catch change / tolerance, 0, 1).
Targets are fixed anchors, not min-max rescaling of the candidate strategies.
Negative profit receives zero profit points. Values above a target saturate.
Role score = sum(objective score x weight) / sum(objective weights).
Overall score = sum(role score x influence) / sum(role influence).

Shared limits independently constrain crossing risk, mean annual profit, mean
supported job-years, and annual survey cost. They do not use the scaled scores.
A strategy passes all stated thresholds only if every included role's score
floor and every enabled shared limit passes. No valid roles means no calculated
agreement. A weighted leader satisfies shared limits but may fail some role
floors. Neither label implies actual stakeholder endorsement or a policy mandate.

The non-dominated label compares risk (lower is better), mean profit (higher),
supported job-years (higher), and mean absolute catch change (lower). A rule is
dominated when another candidate is no worse on all four and better on at least
one. This is relative to the eight candidates and those four metrics; it does
not incorporate every possible objective or uncertainty about preferences.

DELIBERATION AND SAVING
Participants enter their own ballots and concerns; the model never votes for
them. A discussion decision and rationale can record disagreement or a request
for revised objectives. Record round snapshots the completed model settings,
economic assumptions, role priorities, ballots, performance and decision. The
latest eight rounds remain in this browser on this origin. Reset workshop
assumptions resets current preferences and accounting, retaining recorded rounds.
Save scenario includes current workshop settings and rounds. Load scenario
restores them; older biological-only scenario files remain supported. Download
workshop report produces a shareable JSON snapshot with all retained rounds.
Download economics CSV provides all-strategy economic metrics and assumptions.
Plot tools enlarge or export the economic figures as SVG.

20-MINUTE CLASSROOM EXERCISE
1. Start with the same stock, historical F, seed and noise. Identify whether the
   highest-catch rule also yields the highest profit. Explain the role of F cost.
2. Halve landed price. Which rules incur losses? Compare annual losses with the
   probability of a negative NPV. Restore price; raise variable costs by 50%.
3. Allocate 80% of catch to the small-vessel segment. Compare supported job-years
   and profit distribution. Why did the biomass and catch futures stay the same?
4. Divide students into fleet, community, conservation and management roles.
   Enter priorities and acceptable scores before interpreting the matrix. Agree
   on a risk ceiling, profit floor, livelihoods floor and public survey budget.
5. Deliberate, enter votes and concerns, then record a decision and rationale.
   Does the weighted leader have unanimous support? If no candidate passes all
   thresholds, which objective, limit or management procedure might be revised?
6. Keep the seed fixed. Reduce survey CV and run the biological simulation again.
   Increase the cost per survey to represent more precise sampling. Compare the
   risk and economic outputs under the same preferences; record a second round.
   This is a sensitivity exercise, not a formal calculation of value of information.

PARTICIPATION IN MSE
Stakeholders can contribute to objectives, performance measures, plausible
scenarios and interpretation throughout the MSE process. This small workshop
illustrates that iterative role; it is not a substitute for a representative,
facilitated engagement process in an actual fishery.
