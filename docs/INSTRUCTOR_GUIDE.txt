OPEN AND EXPLORE
Open index.html directly in a modern browser, or use the public classroom site.
The downloaded page needs no account, installation, network or server. Text size

On a small screen, use Edit … settings to open the controls.

SEVEN STEPS
1. FISH STOCK: choose an anchovy-like, hake-like or rockfish-like life history.
   Drag annual fishing-pressure bars or use a historical F preset. Historical F
   directly drives age-specific survival, Baranov catch and Beverton–Holt mean
   recruitment. The final historical population starts every future strategy.
2. SURVEY: choose Index + age samples, Index only, or Age samples only. Adjust
   index noise, fish aged per visit and visit frequency. More survey settings
   controls the youngest sampled age, true survey q and index bias.
3. ASSESSMENT: the default is an age-structured assessment. Leave collected ages
   out of the fit to demonstrate their contribution. Inspect estimated SSB and
   the approximate 80% range. Open How well does the model fit the survey? for
   observed/fitted index and age plots. Assessment assumptions allows a wrong
   natural death rate or q, and changes in model flexibility.
4. FISHING RULES: compare three fixed catches, a fixed share of estimated SSB,
   stock-responsive catch rules and FMSY fishing targets. Only settings relevant
   to the selected rule are shown. Optional reference-point detail explains FMSY.
5. COMPARE: inspect catch against the chance of a low stock across eight rules.
   Extra panels contain trajectories, tables, risk intervals and a pinned-run
   comparison. A pin lasts for the page session; change one assumption at a time.
6. ONE FUTURE: Previous/Next, a year number and Play years follow the annual loop.
   True SSB, the relative survey index, age sample, estimated SSB, catch advice
   and realized catch are distinct. This is the first replicate, not the median.
7. PEOPLE & MONEY: change prices, fishing costs or fleet catch shares. Then use
   Stakeholder discussion to set group objectives, shared limits and votes.
   See ECONOMICS_AND_WORKSHOP.txt for the accounting and discussion exercise.

WHAT THE SURVEY MEASURES
An abundance index is a relative measure, not an observation of SSB in tonnes.
Here the index is q times biomass selected by the survey, with mean-one lognormal
error. Ages are sampled in proportion to selected numbers of fish, not weight.
Both products can come from the same survey visit, but their errors differ:
index CV controls index noise; effective sample size controls age-sampling noise.
Survey and fishing selectivity can differ. The oldest age includes older fish.

Index-only visits still cost one survey visit; ages-only visits also count.
The economics panel does not yet add a separate cost for ageing more fish.

WHAT THE ASSESSMENT DOES
It predicts numbers at age from its own previous fitted state, perfectly
reported previous total catch, survival and expected Beverton–Holt recruitment.
It then fits log numbers at age to the current index and/or multinomial age
sample, with penalties around the prediction. Observed ages can change the age
pattern, not just the total scale. With no new survey, it predicts forward.
Estimated ages and SSB feed the fishing rule; true abundance and true F do not.
The lagged F estimate is the Baranov rate that explains the previous catch.
A warning flags a previous catch that the predicted population cannot explain
within F <= 3/year.

This is a simplified SEQUENTIAL penalized-likelihood assessment for teaching.
It does not refit the full historical time series. Growth, maturity, survey and
fishing selectivity, recruitment settings and unfished SSB are assumed known.
q is assumed, not fitted: a relative index and age proportions alone cannot
identify both absolute abundance and q. The assessment assumes zero index bias.
Change assumed M or q to explore model error without changing historical truth.

Two clearly labelled index shortcuts are comparators. Latest value and Last 3
values convert indices using assumed q and a fixed unfished age pattern. They
ignore age samples and catch when estimating SSB. A perfect index can therefore
still give an incorrect SSB if age structure differs from that assumption.
These shortcuts do not calculate an assessment uncertainty range.

TWO DIFFERENT SHADED RANGES
Assessment: an approximate conditional 80% range around one future's estimated
SSB. It uses a local Gaussian information approximation; prediction discards
cross-age covariance and treats catch-derived F as fixed. It excludes uncertainty
in q, biology and model structure and can be too narrow under misspecification.
It is not a coverage guarantee and does not show the distribution across futures.

One future / Compare: pointwise 10th–90th percentiles ACROSS simulated futures.
The median is the middle outcome, not the mean or an assessment confidence band.

A 20-MINUTE DATA-TO-DECISION EXERCISE
1. Keep medium-lived fish, historical F and the random seed fixed. Use both data
   products, 100 aged fish and some index noise. Find the age sample and the
   fitted SSB. Identify which quantities are observed and which are estimated.
2. Leave ages out of the assessment, keeping the survey unchanged. Explain why
   its age pattern, SSB estimate and F-based catch advice can change.
3. Increase aged fish from 20 to 500 with the same index CV. Which observations
   become more precise? Now increase index noise while holding sample size fixed.
4. Survey every 3 years. Use Next survey year and inspect an intervening year.
   Does the assessment predict stock change between visits? What happens to its
   range, and how does an index shortcut behave instead?
5. Double assumed q while keeping true survey q fixed. An assessment can fit the
   index well and still estimate the wrong absolute SSB. Discuss why and examine
   the selected fishing rule's catch advice and risk.
6. Ask stakeholder groups whether more age sampling or a more cautious rule is
   preferable. Discuss the information limits before recording a class decision.

BIOLOGY, TIMING AND MANAGEMENT
SSB is total mature-fish weight: sum(numbers x weight x maturity), in tonnes.
Survivors age annually; the terminal plus group retains older survivors.
Mean age-1 recruitment next year is Beverton–Holt:
 R = 4 h R0 SSB / [SSB0(1-h) + (5h-1)SSB].
History starts unfished at SSB0 = 1,000 t and uses deterministic mean recruitment.
Future recruitment varies around that mean with independent mean-one lognormal
error. Historical F is a forcing input, not an HCR. Future HCRs produce a quota;
implementation error changes intended catch, and true abundance determines the
realized fishing rate. F is capped at 3/year, so an impossible catch is unfilled.

At each future year's START: survey -> assessment -> fishing decision. Then:
catch implementation -> survival/ageing/recruitment -> next year's stock.
Only past reported catch is available when the current decision is made.
Historical observations condition the assessment before the handoff. Strategies
share copies of each replicate's conditioned state and common random draws.

Fixed catches A/B/C are tonne quotas. Proportional catch is U x estimated SSB.
The catch ramp and F ramp reduce fishing between the stock trigger and limit.
The smoothed catch ramp limits annual advice changes, with closure and reopening
exceptions. FMSY maximizes equilibrium age-structured BH yield under assumed
biology; MSY is that yield in tonnes. An F target is converted to quota using
estimated ages. Actual F can differ because estimates and implementation err.

Risk is the fraction of futures with true SSB below the limit at least once in
projection years 1 through the selected horizon. It excludes historical years
and the projection-start state. Zero simulated crossings does not prove zero
risk; optional tables report a 95% Monte Carlo interval. Mean catch includes
transients and is not equilibrium MSY. Horizon changes the risk question.

SAVE, EXPORT AND REPRODUCE
Auto update waits briefly after editing; switch it off to batch changes.
The stale banner means plots still describe the last completed run.
Save/Load scenario includes biological, survey, assessment and workshop settings.
Older filter scenarios map to the new assessment. Earlier browser preferences
retain their other settings while migrating to the age-structured default.

Survey and Assessment share the selected decision year. Next survey year skips
to the next scheduled visit and wraps at the end. Main plots follow one possible
future for the chosen fishing rule. Enlarge/SVG buttons work on each plot.
Download assessment & survey data gives one row per year and age for historical
and future first-replicate data, fitted values, estimated ages/SSB/ranges, lagged
catch/F, fit diagnostics and the complete configuration. True SSB is explicitly
marked teaching-only. Summary CSV includes all survey and assessment controls;
historical CSV gives the raw index in relative units, not tonnes of SSB.

Scientific equations, variance approximations, tests and source links are in
MODEL.md. The presets are illustrative, not assessments of real fisheries.
Economics is accounting after the biological simulation and does not change
fish populations or fleet behavior. Jobs are a landings-supported proxy.
