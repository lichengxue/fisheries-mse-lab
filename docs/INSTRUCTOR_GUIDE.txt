OPEN THE DEMO
Open Classroom_Age_Structured_MSE_Demo.html in a modern browser. No installation,
internet connection or server is needed. The initial run happens automatically.
The online and offline versions use identical code.

THE MODEL'S TWO PHASES
1. HISTORICAL POPULATION DYNAMICS
   Start with the unfished age distribution at SSB0 = 1,000 tonnes. Apply the
   supplied annual historical F series directly to age-specific survival and
   Baranov catch. Beverton–Holt recruitment supplies age 1 in the next year.
   The final historical numbers-at-age vector and spawning biomass form the
   true starting population for every future management strategy. There is no
   manual depletion target or rescaling at the handoff.
2. FUTURE MANAGEMENT STRATEGY EVALUATION
   From that historical endpoint, simulate noisy surveys, estimate population
   state, apply a chosen HCR, implement fishing, and update the stock repeatedly.
   Recruitment varies around the SAME Beverton–Holt mean in the projection.

EDITING HISTORICAL F
In Population dynamics, load an example history or edit Historical F time series.
Enter 1–150 annual values, oldest first, separated by commas, spaces or newlines.
F is instantaneous mortality per year on fully selected ages; accepted values
are 0–3/year. Enter F values only, without a year column. For example:
 0, 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.4, 0.2, 0.1
This supplies eight years. Entry 1 acts between historical year 0 and 1; entry 8
acts between years 7 and 8. The historical stock graph shows states at boundaries
0 through 8. Projection year 0 is the final historical state at boundary 8.
Click Update simulation after editing.

Examples are increasing fishing (30 years), heavy fishing followed by lower F,
constant F = 0.20/year, and unfished history. They are illustrative histories,
not actual assessment data. The first two plots show F input and population
response. Historical series CSV exports F, catch, mean recruitment, starting and
ending SSB, and one replicate's survey and estimate.

BEVERTON–HOLT RECRUITMENT AND AGE DYNAMICS
Mean recruitment to age 1 next year:
 R = 4 h R0 SSB / [SSB0(1-h) + (5h-1)SSB].
This produces R0 at SSB0 and h R0 at 20% SSB0. Steepness h controls compensation.
History uses the expected BH recruitment deterministically to make the effect of
F clear. The recruitment-CV slider controls mean-one lognormal variability around
this relationship in FUTURE projections. Historical F and recruitment contain no
implementation or process noise in this classroom version.

Surviving numbers move from age a to age a+1:
 N[a+1,t+1] = N[a,t] exp[-M - F[t] selectivity[a]].
The terminal age is a plus group retaining surviving older fish. Weight follows
cubic von Bertalanffy growth; maturity is logistic; fishing selectivity is
knife-edge by age. SSB = sum(N x weight x maturity). Presets illustrate an
anchovy-like, hake-like and rockfish-like life history.

HISTORICAL ESTIMATOR CONDITIONING
At the beginning of history the manager assumes its own unfished population.
Noisy historical spawning-biomass surveys and perfectly reported historical
catches condition the estimator throughout history. Survey errors differ across
replicates, but true historical population dynamics are the same. The estimator
never receives true age classes or prescribed historical F. Each future rule
starts from a copy of that replicate's conditioned estimated state, together
with the common true historical endpoint. Survey timing continues across the
handoff. Historical and future survey random streams are separate and repeatable.

Estimation choices: latest survey, up-to-three-survey arithmetic mean, or a simple
age-structured filter. The filter predicts using its own age structure, assumed M,
expected BH recruitment and reported catch; it is not a full fitted assessment.
Direct and averaged-survey methods carry their latest value between surveys.
Known survey catchability q=1 and known SSB0 are simplifying assumptions.
The manager assumes zero survey bias; the bias slider changes the true survey.

EIGHT FUTURE STRATEGIES
- Fixed catch A, B and C: independent user-selected tonnes/year.
- Proportional catch: U x estimated SSB.
- Biomass catch ramp: U x estimated SSB x r.
- Smoothed catch ramp: the preceding ramp with a cap on annual advice changes.
  The last historical catch is the baseline in future year 1. Closure overrides
  smoothing; reopening uses raw ramp advice. With a positive previous catch,
  the cap can require gradual reductions even when U is reduced to zero.
- Constant F target: alpha x management-model FMSY.
- F-based biomass ramp: alpha x management-model FMSY x r.
Here r = clamp[(estimated SSB/SSB0 - limit)/(trigger - limit), 0, 1].
U, F and catch in tonnes are different quantities. The FMSY multiplier alpha is
adjustable. F targets have a ceiling of 3/year.

F targets become catch advice through the Baranov equation using estimated age
structure. The filter supplies its own age estimate. The direct and averaged
survey methods use an assumed unfished age shape scaled to estimated SSB.
Future intended catch receives implementation error; actual F is then solved
against TRUE abundance. Thus realized F can differ from target F. Quotas exceeding
the F = 3/year ceiling remain partly unfilled. Historical F is applied directly
and does not use this HCR-to-quota inversion.

REFERENCE POINTS AND OUTPUTS
Deterministic equilibrium yield is calculated from age-specific survivorship,
BH equilibrium recruitment and Baranov catch. Grid search plus refinement finds
FMSY, the F maximizing this yield; MSY is its catch and SSBMSY its spawning biomass.
Management reference points use assumed M. The F time-series plot also shows
true-model FMSY for teaching. Historical F changes stock condition at the handoff;
it does not alter biological equilibrium reference points when biology is fixed.

The table reports future crossing risk, mean actual catch, mean absolute annual
catch change (tonnes), and median final SSB/SSB0. Risk counts true SSB below the
limit at least once in projection years 1 through the horizon. Historical years
and projection-start state are not included in this risk count. Risk depends on
horizon; zero observed crossings does not prove zero risk. Hover for a 95% Monte
Carlo interval. Mean catch includes transients and may exceed equilibrium MSY over
a finite projection. A fixed quota equal to MSY is not guaranteed safe with noise.

Future biomass and catch bands show pointwise medians and 10th–90th percentiles
across simulated futures, not confidence intervals for the median. The survey,
age, F, management-year and highlighted recruitment plots use the first future.
Historical recruits (blue) lie on the BH mean curve; future recruits (gray) scatter
around it. The orange point follows the selected projection decision year.

All strategies share common random shocks and the same historical true endpoint.
Hold the seed fixed to compare scenarios. New random futures changes observation
and future process/implementation shocks, but not deterministic historical truth.
Summary CSV records the full F input series, settings, reference points and scores.
The browser remembers the latest completed scenario on that origin.

A SHORT CLASSROOM EXERCISE
1. Compare unfished history with increasing F. Explain why their projection-start
   biomass and age composition differ despite the same BH relationship.
2. Paste an F series that rises and then falls. Inspect the historical stock and
   recruitment response; compare recovery across the three life histories.
3. Change h while keeping F fixed. Explain how recruitment compensation affects
   historical stock decline and recovery.
4. From the same historical population, compare fixed catches and the F rules.
5. Increase survey noise and change estimators. Separate true stock changes from
   differences in what the manager observes and estimates.

BOUNDARIES AND SOURCE
Illustrative combined-sex single stock, fixed growth/maturity/selectivity, no
movement, no age-composition sampling, a simplified economic accounting extension (see ECONOMICS_AND_WORKSHOP.txt). Historical years use mean
recruitment, and future recruitment deviations are independent. These are not
calibrated stock assessments or real management advice. Equations and primary
source links are in the demo's expandable model section.

src/model.js: pure population, historical F, BH, estimation, HCR and reference
point modules. historicalPopulation() generates truth; conditionEstimator()
uses historical data; runEnsemble() branches into future strategies.
src/app.js: controls, web worker, SVG plots, CSV export.
src/page.html: interface, assumptions, equations and source links.
scripts/build.cjs: creates index.html and dist/index.html.
tests/model.test.cjs: 21 model and stochastic consistency checks.

Edit source; run: npm run build
Verify: node --test tests/model.test.cjs
Only Node built-in modules are required. Open index.html directly, or run npm start
and open http://127.0.0.1:8000. The GitHub Pages workflow deploys dist/.
